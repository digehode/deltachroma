Today I made some actual progress on the tool rather than just playing with the repository and CI/CD pipeline.  Nothing big in terms of functionality (of which there is still none) but some decisions made about libraries and UI elements.

## Colour Libraries

Not a lot to write on this other than a decision was made to use [Color.js](https://colorjs.io/).

I also considered [Colorizr](https://github.com/gilbarbara/colorizr). If the focus was more about checking contrast or creating uniform gradients, this would have been the choice, but it looks like Color.js is the better choice for the maths I need.  The accessibility and palette generation bit is what I'm building, so a lot of the benefits of Colorizr won't be as important for this project.

[colordx](https://github.com/dkryaklin/colordx) was another good option, particularly for the colour perception deficiency simulation. But Colors.js has built-in $\Delta_E$ calculations that colordx doesn't.

## Colour Picker

This was more difficult.  There are so many, and I struggled to be able to work out which will fit what I need because so much is undecided.  How do you decide what features you need, what patterns would just not work, when you don't know what those will be yet?

In the end, I went with something popular and clean, favouring accessibility over other features. I had hoped to use an OKLCH colour picker, but I couldn't find one that was small enough to be part of what will already be quite a busy interface, claimed to be accessible, etc.

I hope [coloris](https://coloris.js.org/) was the right choice, but this at least is an area that can probably be redone at any point in the future with minimal disruption as long as I keep everything sensible.
